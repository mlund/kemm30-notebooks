### Experimental interpretation, exercises and reading

The aggregation landscape is intentionally illustrative. It combines a screened repulsive exponential with a fixed attractive exponential. Its amplitudes are selected to show how a barrier can fall when salt increases. It is not a complete DLVO calculation, and its ordinate must not be converted into an exact aggregation probability. Protein charge patterns, pH, specific ion binding, hydration, steric layers, hydrophobic attraction and conformational changes can change the outcome. Gels add Donnan partitioning and network elasticity. A useful experimental comparison controls pH, temperature, particle or protein concentration and salt identity.

The optional conceptual distinction between linearized PB and full PB matters. Linearized PB assumes $|z_i e\psi/(k_BT)|\ll1$. Full PB keeps the Boltzmann nonlinearity but still treats ions through a mean field and solvent as a continuum. It does not automatically represent ion correlations, finite ion sizes, hydration or specific adsorption. The dependence of these effects on system and observable prevents a universal salt cutoff.

### Teaching route and timing

The main lecture has 17 slides followed by six supporting notes pages. The opening is: 1 applications; 2 ion atmosphere; 3 planar cation/anion profiles; 4 numerical iteration animation; 5 Poisson + Boltzmann and the nonlinear PB equation; 6 linearization; 7 plane/sphere/cylinder comparison; 8 ionic strength and Debye length for 1:1, 1:2 and 2:2 salts. Slides 9–11 give planar/spherical worked examples; 12–14 cover screened pair and contact energies; 15–17 cover aggregation, limitations and summary. For 45 minutes, allow 4 minutes for slides 1–2, 10 for 3–5, 8 for 6–8, 8 for 9–11, 8 for 12–14 and 5 for 15–17, plus 2 minutes for transitions. Interaction time is included. The supporting notes follow the main lecture; the motivation page retains its existing skip setting. No answer gates progression.
