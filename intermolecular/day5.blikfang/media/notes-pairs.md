### Screened pair interactions and colloidal contact

For two ideal point charges at centre separation $R$, the unscreened Coulomb energy is $U_C(R)/(k_BT)=z_1z_2\ell_B/R$. The screened Coulomb energy, also called the Yukawa energy, is $U_Y(R)=U_C(R)e^{-R/\ell_D}$. Thus C denotes Coulomb and Y denotes Yukawa. Here $\kappa=1/\ell_D$. For zero salt the Yukawa expression reduces to Coulomb's law. Increasing salt decreases the magnitude for either sign of $z_1z_2$. Screening alone cannot reverse the sign. The ratio $U_Y/U_C=e^{-\kappa R}$ is an energy ratio, not a force ratio: differentiating the energy would add a $(1+\kappa R)$ factor to the force ratio.

The reused day 4 explorer has two explicit modes. Point-charge mode uses $a=0$ in the amplitude and restricts plotted and dragged separations to at least 4 Å to avoid a singularity. Finite-sphere mode has $a=2$ Å. Its potential map sums the isolated-sphere fields with amplitude $Z_{\rm eff}=Ze^{\kappa a}/(1+\kappa a)$; its pair curve uses the product of the two effective valences. This is a linear superposition illustration, not an exact solution for interacting excluded-volume spheres. In each mode, the map and curve use the same amplitude convention. Dashed circles are distance markers at one Debye length, not boundaries of the atmosphere. The water permittivity is fixed at 78.4. The rounded values $\ell_B=7.1$ Å and $\ell_D=3.04/\sqrt I$ Å match the other slides.

The finite-sphere interaction in the supplied exam problem is

$$\frac{w(R)}{k_BT}=Z^2\ell_B\frac{e^{-\kappa(R-2a)}}{R(1+\kappa a)^2},\qquad Q=Ze.$$

At contact $R=2a$, the exponential becomes one. For the lecture target $w(2a)=0.1\,k_BT$, substitute $w(2a)/(k_BT)=0.1$ directly and solve $1+\kappa a=\sqrt{Z^2\ell_B/(2a\times0.1)}$. With $Z=3$, $a=30$ Å and $\ell_B=7.1$ Å, this gives $1+\kappa a=3.26$, $\kappa=0.0754$ Å$^{-1}$, $\ell_D=13.3$ Å and $I=0.053$ M (53 mM). No extra symbol is needed for the numerical target. Inverting $\kappa$ gives $I/(1\,\mathrm M)=(3.04\,\mathrm{\AA}\times\kappa)^2$.

A nonnegative salt threshold exists only if the stipulated contact repulsion does not exceed its zero-salt value $Z^2\ell_B/(2a)$. If the particles are already below the target at zero salt, added salt cannot restore that repulsion within this model. The lecture target is chosen to demonstrate inversion, not as a universal criterion of colloidal stability. Its corresponding isolated-sphere surface potential is $\Phi_a=Z\ell_B/[a(1+\kappa a)]\approx0.218$, consistent with a weak-potential approximation.
